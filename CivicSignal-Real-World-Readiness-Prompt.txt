## Product and users

CivicSignal lets residents report non-emergency local issues, then follow the responsible public-service organization’s response. Its core path is:

Resident reports → responsible organization receives it → verified official takes action and posts updates → resident can track the outcome.

There are three distinct experiences:
- Residents submit and track their own reports.
- Verified public-service and law-enforcement officials review authorized reports, take ownership, update statuses, and communicate progress.
- Administrators configure organizations and categories, verify officials, moderate reports, and review audit history.

Make the complete report lifecycle work end to end:
- Use CivicSignal's built-in AI to determine the best configured department for a submitted report, using the report details and available jurisdiction/category information. Show the selected department and routing outcome clearly. If the AI cannot make a reliable selection, do not silently guess or discard the report; explain what needs attention and provide an appropriate review or fallback path.
- Deliver routed reports to the authorized department workflow. An authorized official can claim ownership, hand the report off to another eligible official or department when appropriate, and post status changes and updates. Record who performed each action and when, and keep the handoff and status history visible to authorized users.
- When an official marks an issue fixed/resolved, let the resident who submitted it respond from their account: the problem is solved, it is still persistent, or they are unsure. Keep this resident feedback distinct from the official status. Make persistent/uncertain feedback visible to the authorized workflow for follow-up; do not silently change the official status.
- Give each resident an account view of all of their reports, current statuses, updates, status history, and any requested follow-up. Residents must be able to return to a report from their account without needing a saved one-off link. If they have enabled notifications, notify them about meaningful report updates and the official's fixed/resolved status; respect their notification preferences and show useful in-app notification state.

This is not an emergency or dispatch service. Do not imply that reports are monitored continuously, guarantee response times, claim integrations that do not exist, or expose private resident or sensitive law-enforcement information.

## Visual direction

Make CivicSignal look like a credible, modern service from a well-run municipality: calm, clear, human, and official without feeling like an old government portal or a generic analytics dashboard. Create a recognizable visual system and apply it consistently across all screens.

Use a restrained civic palette built around deep forest green, warm off-white surfaces, readable dark ink, and muted secondary colors. Reserve amber and red for meaningful warnings and errors. Make report statuses easy to scan, distinguishable by both text and color, and consistent everywhere. Use a deliberate type scale, comfortable line lengths, clear headings, generous but efficient spacing, subtle borders, and restrained shadows. Use the existing icon library only where an icon improves recognition; do not use emoji as interface icons. Avoid decorative gradients, oversized marketing sections inside signed-in workflows, excessive cards, dense tiny text, and motion that distracts.

Give the brand and page headers a clear hierarchy. Make the primary action obvious on every screen. Keep navigation compact and predictable, show the active location, make notification counts understandable, and make role-specific navigation clear. The app should feel like one product across onboarding, authentication, dashboards, forms, report detail, notifications, profile, and administration.

Pay special attention to these screens:
- **Resident home:** make “Report an issue” the obvious next action. Present reports as readable, scannable items with reference, issue, location/category where available, current status, and last update. Make first-use empty state welcoming and helpful.
- **Report form:** group fields in a sensible order, explain what information helps staff respond, clarify optional photo use and privacy, show validation beside the relevant field, and prevent accidental duplicate submissions while saving. Make the emergency limitation visible before submission.
- **Report detail:** make reference code, status, location, responsible organization, and latest update easy to find. Show a legible progress/history timeline and official messages. Let the submitting resident provide the solved/persistent/unsure confirmation after an official marks the issue fixed, and show that feedback separately from the official status. Present actions only to authorized roles and make status changes and public-facing messages deliberate and clear.
- **Official queue:** optimize for reviewing and acting on many reports, with useful search and filters, clear priority through information hierarchy (do not fabricate urgency), readable status, AI-selected department, ownership, and handoff history, and useful no-results/loading/error states. Make claiming and handing off reports clear and attributable. Keep key actions usable on narrow screens.
- **Resident account and notifications:** make the resident's complete report list, report histories, current statuses, updates, and follow-up feedback available from their account. Make enabled notifications useful and consistent with in-app report history, and provide clear notification-preference controls.
- **Admin:** keep verification, organization/category setup, moderation, and audit history organized and legible. Use responsive table alternatives or well-designed horizontal scrolling on mobile, clear destructive-action labeling, and confirmation for irreversible actions.
- **Setup and sign-in/sign-up:** explain what each account type means, especially that official access requires administrator verification. Make the first administrator setup and account-pending/suspended states understandable and calm.

## Real-world behavior and trust

A polished page is not enough: verify that controls lead to real, persisted actions through the existing API and that the UI recovers correctly when something fails.

- Keep server-side authorization authoritative. Never rely on hidden buttons to protect data or official actions. Preserve separation among residents, verified officials, law enforcement, and administrators.
- Ensure users can understand where a report goes. Use only configured categories and organizations; do not claim a department was notified unless the API confirms successful routing. If a required organization/category is missing or inactive, explain the problem plainly and give an appropriate next step rather than silently misrouting or discarding a report.
- Integrate AI-assisted department selection with the actual report workflow rather than presenting it as decorative advice. Base routing only on configured departments and supported report/jurisdiction data, make the routing result visible, preserve a clear failure/review path, and ensure the backend persists and enforces the final department assignment.
- Make official ownership, handoffs, status changes, and resident fixed-status feedback persistent, attributable, and visible in the appropriate report history. Enforce who can claim, hand off, update, and respond on the server; a resident's persistent/unsure feedback must trigger a clear follow-up signal without impersonating an official or silently rewriting the official status.
- Keep each resident's reports and complete update history accessible from their signed-in account. Send report-update notifications only when the resident has enabled the relevant notification channel; handle delivery failures honestly and retain in-app history so notifications are not the only way to follow a report.
- Preserve clear confirmation after report submission, including the reference code, and make it easy to return to the report later.
- Handle pending verification, suspended accounts, inaccessible reports, expired sessions, network/API errors, validation errors, duplicate clicks, empty data, and slow loading with helpful UI. Retain form entries after recoverable errors.
- Keep the emergency disclaimer visible and unambiguous. Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive data. Do not expose a resident’s contact details or private reports to other residents. Treat uploaded images as untrusted input and preserve existing server-side validation and authorization.
- Use plain language. Explain what each status means and what the resident can expect next, without inventing service-level promises. Make timestamps and reference identifiers readable.
- Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic headings and form labels, screen-reader announcements for important results, sufficient color contrast, zoom, and reduced-motion preferences.
- Make layouts work from small phones through large desktop screens. Prevent clipped controls, horizontal page overflow, unreadable tables, and tiny tap targets. Forms and actions should remain comfortable to use on touch devices.
- Keep content realistic and useful; do not add fake metrics, fake report data, placeholder departments, pretend maps, or decorative features that imply unavailable functionality.
